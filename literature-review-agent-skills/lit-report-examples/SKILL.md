# Worked examples for the investigation report

Each example shows a weak passage, a better one, and why the second is better. The geology in them is invented. Take the reasoning, not the wording: a report written by copying these sentences will read as a copy.

The examples leave out tags and markers, except where a marker is the point. `lit-investigation-report` gives those.

## 1. The answer paragraph (your notes, before the outline)

**Weak.** "The investigation looked at the seismic data and the wells. Several products were made. Some results are uncertain. More work is needed."

**Better.** "The investigation sought to address whether the Halden sand is thick and continuous enough between the two wells to plan an injector. The analysis found that it is present at both wells (18 m and 31 m) and can be followed on seismic across the northern two thirds of the area. South of the incised channel it cannot be followed, so continuity there is not established. The outcome rests on the well ties and on the amplitude map; it would change if the southern reflector is a different unit."

**Why.** The better one names the question, gives the answer, names what carries it, and says what would change it. Everything in the report can now be tested against it: a paragraph that serves none of these four things has no place.

## 2. A results paragraph

**Weak.** "Porosity was calculated. The values are variable. Core data were also used. The result is provisional. Figure 4."

**Better.** "The sand is more porous in the upper unit than in the lower. Log porosity averages 0.21 ± 0.02 in the upper unit and 0.14 ± 0.03 in the lower, and the eleven core plugs agree with the logs to within 0.01 after the depth shift. As [[fig:poro-depth]] shows, the change is abrupt at the shale break at 1,842 m, not gradual with depth, which points to a change of facies and not to compaction. Core covers the upper unit only, so the lower value rests on logs alone."

**Why.** The first sentence is the point. The numbers have uncertainties and a place. The figure is read for the reader, and the reading leads to what it means. The limitation says exactly what it limits.

## 3. Reading a figure

**Weak.** "The depth map was produced. Figure 6 Figure 7."

**Better.** "The surface dips to the south-west at about 3° and is cut by one fault that trends north-west ([[fig:depth-map]]). The blank cells in the south-east corner are where the reflector could not be followed."

A published survey report (U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 1900) does it in two sentences: "The potential for a viable hydrogen source is shown as a COS map in figure 10A. This map illustrates the important role that the North American craton plays in providing a general source of old ... granitoids."

**Also weak.** "Figure 8 and Figure 9 show the fault on two more sections, and Figure 10 shows the time map." The sentence names three figures and reads none. If the two sections add nothing to the first, they are not in the report.

**Why.** A marker at the end of a paragraph says only that a figure exists. Say what the figure shows: the trend, the structure, the anomaly, the scale, and what its blanks mean. A figure that you cannot read for the reader in a sentence is a figure the argument does not need.

## 4. Methods as a procedure, with reasons

**Weak.** "The specialist ran a tracker. The threshold was 0.8. The window was 8 samples. The search was 5 samples. A second run was made. The specialist then ran a converter."

**Better.** "The top of the sand was followed across the volume with a waveform-correlation tracker [[m:...]], seeded at the two well ties. A correlation tracker was chosen because the reflector keeps its shape where its amplitude fades, which an amplitude tracker would lose. Picks were accepted where the correlation with the seed exceeded the threshold in [[tab:parameters]]; cells below it were left blank and not filled. An amplitude tracker was tried first and set aside: it jumped one cycle across the channel margin."

**Why.** The settings are in the table, so the prose can say why the method was chosen, what was done with weak picks, and what was tried and dropped. A reader could repeat it. Nothing in it is a result.

## 5. Defining a term, and what a number does not mean

**Weak.** "The Q2 test failed at both wells and the score is 0.4."

**Better.** "At neither well did the log porosity agree with the core plugs to within the tolerance of the comparison, so the log porosity is not yet calibrated." And, for a score: "The favourability score runs from 0 to 1. It ranks areas against each other; it is not the probability that a well would find the resource."

The same report says of its own score: "a source COS of 0.5 does not imply that 50 percent of wells in a particular location will successfully observe hydrogen."

**Why.** The reader does not know a tool's code for a test. Say what the test checks. For any score or chance, say what it measures and the wrong reading it invites.

## 6. A limitation, said once

**Weak.** The words "the two surveys differ in bandwidth" in most paragraphs of the Results.

**Better.** Once, where amplitude first matters: "The volume joins two surveys of different bandwidth along a seam that runs east to west. This affects every comparison of amplitude across the seam, so the amplitude map is read separately north and south of it. It does not affect the picked times or the shape of the surface." After that, a few words where a reader could be misled ("south of the seam, on its own scale"), and nothing elsewhere.

**Also weak.** "measured from an unconfirmed datum" after each depth, in most sections. A limit that touches every result is said once, where the depths are introduced: "All depths in this report are measured below a datum that the investigation could not confirm. A confirmed datum would move every depth by the same amount and would not change the shape of a surface." After that the depths are plain numbers.

**Why.** Said once, with what it changes and what it leaves standing, a limitation informs. Said everywhere, it hides which results can be used.

## 7. Findings when the question stays open

**Weak.** "The correlation could not be completed. The data were insufficient. The result is unresolved."

**Better.** As findings, each one sentence:
1. "The sand is present at both wells, at 18 m and 31 m thick." (established)
2. "Compaction does not explain the porosity contrast between the two units." (established: a reading ruled out)
3. "The sand is continuous across the northern two thirds of the area." (interpreted, from the amplitude map)
4. "Continuity south of the incised channel cannot be determined from the supplied data. The reflector there may be the same sand or a younger unit that fills the channel; a well tie or a dated sample south of the channel would decide."

**Why.** "Could not be determined" is a finding when it is exact: what is unknown, the candidates that remain, and the test that would choose between them. A thing ruled out is a finding too. Together these tell the reader what to do next.

## 8. A lead that was dropped

**Weak.** Three paragraphs on a second sand that the investigation did not pursue, or no mention of it at all.

**Better.** "A thinner sand about 40 m above the Halden sand was not assessed further: it is present at one well only, and it is below the resolution of the seismic data."

**Why.** One sentence, with the reason. The reader sees that it was looked at and why it was left.

## 9. A discussion that argues

**Weak.** "The results have limitations. The amplitude map has a seam. Continuity is unresolved. Further work is recommended."

**Better.** "Taken together, the results support a sand that is continuous in the north and leave the south open. The well ties and the amplitude map agree in the north: the sand is seen at both wells and the reflector between them keeps one character. The reflector south of the channel is weaker and lies 40 ms deeper. The same sand, sagging over compacted channel fill, would explain that, but so would a younger unit that fills the channel. The two readings predict different things: the same sand should keep its thickness across the channel, and a younger fill should thin toward the channel margins. The thickness map is too noisy near the channel to tell, which is why a tie south of it matters more than any reprocessing."

**Why.** It combines results, sets two readings against each other, says what each predicts, and ends on what would decide. A list of limitations is not a discussion.

## 10. Conclusions

**Weak.** The paragraph that opens the Conclusions, then the same paragraph again; or "The injector is not recommended because the sand is thin" when the body showed that the sand could not be followed south of the channel.

**Better.** Three short paragraphs: the answer with its real reason; each hypothesis by its content with its status; what would settle the open items, the most decisive first.

**Why.** The conclusion carries the reason the body gave, not an easier one. It says each thing once.

## 11. Answering a renderer's note

**The note.** "3 claims are carried by no Results or Discussion block."

**Weak answer.** Add a paragraph at the end of Results: "In addition, the plug permeabilities are from the upper unit only. The second well has no pressure data. The grain-size trend is not statistically tested."

**Better answer.** Find where each claim belongs in the outline. The permeability claim belongs with the porosity results, as the sentence that says how far the upper unit's values can stand for the lower. The pressure claim belongs where continuity is argued, as a test that could not be made. The grain-size claim belongs with the facies reading, as the reason that reading is only partly supported. Rewrite those three paragraphs so that each claim does its work there.

**Why.** Three claims with no home means the outline missed a subject or split one. Sentences added at the end pass the check and tell the reader nothing, because nothing connects them to an argument.

**The note.** "Figure `tie-b` is not referred to in the text."

**Weak answer.** Add "[[fig:tie-b]]" to the end of the nearest paragraph.

**Better answer.** Ask whether the paragraph needs the figure. If it does, write the sentence that reads it. If no paragraph needs it, take the figure out.

## 12. Placing the reader

**Weak.** "A closure was found on the depth grid, with a relief of 22 m and an area of 0.41 km². The fault lies 180 m from the first well."

**Better.** "The mapped surface is a reflector about 90 m above the top of the Halden sand: it was followed because the sand top itself gives no continuous reflection. On that surface the ground rises to a closed high about 1.2 km north-east of the first well; the second well lies outside it, 600 m down the southern flank ([[fig:base-map]]). The high has 22 m of relief and covers 0.41 km². Whether the sand top has the same shape 90 m below is not shown."

**Why.** The weak one gives numbers that the reader cannot place: which surface, how far from the reservoir, where from the wells. The better one says where the feature is, what it was mapped on, and how that level relates to the level the decision is about. The gap between the two levels is then plain, and it is the main limit of the result.

## 13. A setting when no regional study was made

**Weak.** "The record contains no regional geology. The regional task did not run, so no basin framework is described."

**Better.** "The wells drilled a succession of marine mudstone with two sandstone units, the Halden sand (top at 1,842 m in the first well and 1,871 m in the second) and the thinner Orrin sand 60 m below it. The supplied fossil datums place the Halden sand in the Upper Cretaceous. In the cored interval of the first well the sand is fine to medium grained with mud drapes. The investigation made no study of the basin, so the structural history and the burial history of the area are not described."

**Why.** The reader needs to know what rock the report is about, even when nothing regional is known. The wells, the tops, the ages and the core already hold a local setting. What is missing is said once.

## 14. The rock, not the software

**Weak.** "The loader rejected the pressure file because its header row was missing, so the gradient tool returned nothing. The second run was cancelled."

**Better.** "The pressure readings of the first well could not be used: the supplied file does not say which column is depth. The fluid contact is therefore taken from the logs alone and has no pressure check."

**Why.** The reader does not know the software and does not need to. Each event is written by what it leaves known and unknown about the rock. The event itself has one place: the record paragraph of the Conclusions.

## 15. The answer to the decision

**Weak.** "The work supports the decision on the injector between the two wells. The evidence does not support a position for it."

**Better.** "The decision is whether to plan an injector between the two wells. The evidence does not yet support one: the sand cannot be followed south of the channel."

**Why.** "Supports the decision" sounds like a yes, and the next sentence then reads as its opposite. Name the decision, then give the answer and its reason.

## 16. A name the investigation coined

**Weak.** "Marker N2 was tracked with 97% coverage. N2 crosses two fossil events."

**Better.** "The sand top gives no continuous reflection, so the investigation followed the nearest strong reflection above it. That reflection has no formation name; this report calls it the upper marker. It was tracked across 97% of the area."

**Why.** A label that the investigation made up means nothing outside it. Say what the thing is, why it was used, and what it will be called.

## 17. Findings the argument does not need

**Weak.** Results end with a section of single sentences: "The grain-size trend was not tested." "The water sample is from the lower unit." "The older map was digitised again."

**Better.** One sentence ("The remaining findings do not change the answer and are listed in [[tab:other]]"), then a table with the columns finding and status, tagged with those claims.

**Why.** A run of one-sentence paragraphs is a list that pretends to be prose, and the reader cannot tell which of them matter. A table says honestly that these are the rest.

## 18. Two records that disagree

**Weak.** "The Halden sand is 14 m thick at the second well, so the injector target is thick enough there. The core description gives 6 m of sand."

**Better.** "The supplied records disagree about the sand at the second well. The completion log gives 14 m of Halden sand. The core description of the same interval gives 6 m of sand and 8 m of mudstone. Neither says how its thickness was measured, so the investigation cannot say which holds, and it treats the thickness at the second well as between 6 and 14 m."

**Why.** When two records disagree, the disagreement is the finding. Writing one of them as fact, and the other a sentence later, leaves the reader with two answers.
