# Geoscience question bank

Use these question sets to turn a broad request into specific, answerable questions. Select only what the task needs; researching irrelevant domains wastes the budget and dilutes the result.

## 1. Geological framework

**Tectonic evolution**
- What basin type is it, and how did it form? What were the extension, compression, strike-slip, inversion and uplift phases, and when did each occur?
- Which fault systems and structural provinces are documented, and how are they dated?

**Stratigraphy**
- What are the formations and their ages? How are those ages constrained (biostratigraphy, radiometric dating, or regional correlation)?
- What unconformities are there, and what correlation schemes and sequence frameworks exist? How has the nomenclature changed over time?

**Sedimentology**
- What depositional environments, facies and systems are documented, and what are the provenance and palaeogeography?
- Which reservoir depositional models are proposed, and on what data?

**Structural geology**
- What are the structural styles and geometries, and which deformation phases and fault kinematics are recorded?
- What evidence is there of fault reactivation and trap-forming structures?

## 2. Petroleum-system elements

Research each element separately, and record the evidence type for each.

| Element | Questions | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Is a source rock documented? What are its richness, kerogen type, maturity and distribution? Is there an oil-to-source correlation? | TOC; Rock-Eval (HI, S2, Tmax); vitrinite reflectance; biomarkers; burial and thermal models |
| Reservoir | Which reservoir units exist? What are their lithology, depositional setting, porosity, permeability, diagenesis, thickness, distribution and connectivity? | Core analysis, petrography, log interpretation, outcrop studies |
| Seal | Which top and lateral seals exist? What are their lithology, thickness, continuity and capacity? Is fault seal considered? | Mercury injection, well lithology, regional mapping, fault-seal analysis |
| Trap | Which trap types are documented: structural, stratigraphic or combination? What are their geometries and closure? | Seismic interpretation, maps, discovery descriptions |
| Migration | What pathways, carrier beds and fault conduits are documented, and what shows and seeps? | Shows, seeps, fluid inclusions, geochemical correlation |
| Charge | Is there evidence of actual charge: discoveries, tests, phase? | Discovery reports, DSTs, production data |
| Timing | When did generation and migration occur relative to trap formation? | Dated trap formation plus modelled or measured generation timing |
| Preservation | Is there evidence of uplift, erosion, breaching, remigration or biodegradation? | Thermochronology, oil geochemistry, structural history |

Rules for petroleum-system research:
- **Structures are not prospects.** Do not call a documented structure a prospect or trap just because it could trap hydrocarbons.
- **Timing needs dated events on both sides.** A charge–trap timing relationship requires both the trap formation and the charge to be dated. Report whichever is missing as unknown.
- **Occurrence needs documentation.** Keep documented hydrocarbon occurrences (discoveries, tests, shows, seeps) separate from inferred ones.

## 3. Other resource types

- **CO₂ storage:**
  - storage-formation capacity and injectivity data;
  - caprock properties and continuity;
  - faults and legacy wells as leakage paths;
  - pressure regime;
  - analogue injection projects.
- **Geothermal:**
  - heat flow and thermal gradient;
  - reservoir permeability, whether matrix, fracture or fault-controlled;
  - fluid chemistry and recharge;
  - cap rock;
  - the structural controls on fluid flow.
- **Mineral systems:**
  - metal and fluid sources;
  - fluid pathways (crustal architecture);
  - the depositional trap, whether physical or chemical;
  - preservation after mineralisation;
  - geodynamic setting;
  - deposit models and analogues.

If the resource type is not covered here, agree the question set with Geo Oracle, via `requires_clarification`, before researching.

## 4. Priority when the budget is tight

Research in this order, and stop at the level the task needs:
1. The elements the task names explicitly, and evidence local to the study area.
2. The frameworks those elements depend on, e.g. the stratigraphic framework behind a source-rock correlation.
3. Evidence of timing and preservation.
4. Major competing interpretations.
5. Background context, only as much as the elements above require.
